## Structural revision (round six): three columns

Andrew restructured the page: three columns, present in header and body.

- **Column 1** (persistent left rail, full height): logo in the header, three short lines in the body (status, contact, milmoe.com CTA).
- **Top of body, columns 2+3 merged:** the identity paragraph, trimmed.
- **Column 2** (wider): a new headline about the actual work, not geography, then the professional narrative: Bay Area agency work through to Chicago hardware/physical AI work. Make:SF is out of this column entirely.
- **Column 3** (narrower): headline "Make:SF," 2 to 3 sentences on what it was, keeping the specific numbers, then the three photos below.

Three specific edits worth flagging:

**The identity paragraph lost its second sentence.** It used to close "One person. Doing the work directly, not managing a team that does it for him." Andrew's words: "Coolaboratory is supposed to be collaborative, not one person." Cut entirely, not reworded, since the whole premise of that sentence (solo operator, no team) was the thing he was objecting to, not just its phrasing. The paragraph now ends at "...support for the maker community."

**"Bay Area to Chicago" is retired as a headline.** Andrew's critique: it "doesn't say anything about Coolaboratory aside from geographic happenstance." He offered "interactive product design" as a direction, not final copy. Applying the headline formula properly here (outcome-led, specific, the "So What?" test) rather than defaulting back to a place-based "X to Y" structure I'd already used once: the actual through-line across both eras isn't geography, it's the discipline itself, applied first to software (agency UX/IA work) and then to hardware (physical AI for startups). New headline: "Interactive product design, on screen and off." It keeps Andrew's direction (names the real discipline, not a category tag), passes the "So What?" test (it says what kind of work this is, not just where it happened), and doesn't reuse the "X to Y" scaffold I'd leaned on for the old heading, so it doesn't just look like the same template with different nouns dropped in. One trade-off to flag for Grant: it's longer than the old four-word heading, seven words against four, worth knowing before locking column 2's width. **Superseded in round ten**, see Column 2's "Heading, final" note below for the current headline.

**Column 1 picked up two more cuts mid-round**, both are redundancy cuts on the same logic Andrew's been applying all along, don't restate a thing and then restate that you'll restate it if it changes:
- Status line loses its second sentence: "If that changes, this page will say so." is cut. Andrew's reasoning: stating a status and then stating you'll update the status if it changes is saying the same thing twice.
- Contact line loses its second sentence: "Real inbox, actually checked." is cut. Andrew's reasoning: nobody publishes an email address they don't intend to check, so the reassurance was redundant with the act of listing it at all.
- CTA line is unchanged: "Hiring for a full-time role, not a project? That's milmoe.com."

The voice and labeling principles from rounds four and five (no category-tag kickers, fragments only where a subject is already established, dry asides that understate something true rather than perform personality) still apply and shaped the new Column 2 and Column 3 copy below. Condensed history of those two rounds is in the appendix at the bottom for reference; the copy below is what's current.

**Status line, final (round seven):** "Email for availability." is Andrew's own phrasing, chosen over three alternates I drafted ("Availability's limited right now. Still honest to the original brief constraint (doesn't say or imply he's actively soliciting contract work), just answers it by inviting a question instead of pre-empting one.

**CTA line, final (round eight):** replaced "Hiring for a full-time role, not a project? That's milmoe.com." with Andrew's own phrasing, "For full-time roles, visit Milmoe.com." His correction: the old heading's framing (on screen and off) wasn't quite right, the real distinction is closer to "on screen and screen connecting to the real world."

**Closing line, reworked (round eleven):** the coherence flag above is resolved. "Taking the work out of the browser and into real machines" is gone, that was the exact "on screen and off" callback Andrew flagged. Replacement pulls from raw material he gave directly, drawn from his milmoe.com positioning: "I design Industrial IoT software optimized for frontline workers in high-stakes physical environments, alongside scalable enterprise SaaS for global operations. My work transforms heavy industrial workflows and massive data models into intuitive, frictionless digital tools." Cut: "optimized," "scalable enterprise SaaS," "intuitive, frictionless," all SaaS-positioning vocabulary that doesn't belong in Coolaboratory's register. ## Appendix: historical rounds (superseded structure, principles carried forward)

**Round four (headline/kicker system):** Andrew rejected the original mono-kicker taxonomy ("WHAT THIS IS," "THE HISTORY," "FIELD NOTES: BAY AREA, C. 2009-2012") as generic and template-feeling, and "In short" as an opening heading. The fix wasn't better label words, it was asking whether a label was doing real work at all. Applied Rule of One, outcome-led headline formulas, and the Five-Second Hook to decide, section by section, that almost nothing needed a label: a category tag above a sentence that already opens by naming the thing directly is scaffolding, not writing. The one label that survived round four was "Bay Area to Chicago" as a real headline (not a kicker) for the old single history section, on the grounds that it was specific and place-based rather than a self-referential category tag. Round six retired that heading anyway, once Andrew pointed out it described geography instead of work, see the main revision note above.

**Round five (voice recalibration):** Andrew sent screenshots of the actual old coolaboratory.com (2011 to 2013) as a voice reference only, not a content source, nothing from it was reused. Signal taken from it: the old site was confident and a little literary, used sentence fragments and colon-led lists for rhythm, and used dry, flatly-stated asides even in formal copy ("Password required."). Round four's strip-down solved the template problem but not the personality problem, stripped down isn't the same as considered. Applied fragments, tighter colon-led lists, and drier phrasing throughout, with two rules: fragments only where a subject was already established the sentence before, and a dry aside understates something true rather than performs personality for its own sake. Most of round five's specific sentences (the old three-paragraph history section, "checked regularly" versus "actually checked," the two-sentence status and contact lines) have since been cut, trimmed, or restructured in round six, but the two rules themselves are still active and were applied again when writing Column 2 and Column 3.
